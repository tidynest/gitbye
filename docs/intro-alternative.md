# GitBye

You know the one.

The follow arrives at twenty past three in the morning. No stars, no issues, no
comment on anything you have ever written, no evidence that a human being has
looked at a single line of your work. Just a follow, from an account whose bio
reads "Building. Shipping. Founder. 10x." and whose pinned repository is a fork
of a 2021 tutorial with the README still saying `your-project-name-here`.

You follow back, because you are a decent person and it costs nothing.

Eleven days later they are gone. Not gone from GitHub, obviously. Gone from
your followers, having pocketed the follow-back on the way out, off to run the
identical routine on somebody three timezones over. You did not notice. That is
the entire design. Nobody notices. Nobody checks.

Go and look at the profile now. Followers: 4,203. Following: 11. The eleven are
their mum, Linus Torvalds, three accounts that post exclusively about AI agents,
and someone who owes them money. That ratio is the product. It is displayed like
a medal, and it was assembled the way a magpie assembles a nest.

The contribution graph tells on them. One green fortnight in 2022, then tundra.
The 400-star repository is an `awesome-` list, which is to say a bulleted list of
links to other people's work, curated in the sense that somebody once pressed
enter.

They call this growth. It is a for-loop with a `sleep()` in it.

The genuinely pathetic part is the arithmetic. To look impressive to strangers,
they have to be quietly rude to several thousand real people, individually, on a
schedule, forever. That is not networking. That is standing in a room shaking
hands while staring over everyone's shoulder for somebody more useful, except
the room is the whole industry and the handshake has been automated.

GitBye just notices. Then it puts your follow back where it belongs, which is
nowhere near them.
